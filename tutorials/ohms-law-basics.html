Ohm's law at the bench

Relate voltage, current, and resistance in a simple resistive DC circuit.

Ohm's law describes the relationship between voltage across a resistive element, current through it, and its resistance: V = I × R. Rearranging gives I = V ÷ R and R = V ÷ I. Use volts, amperes, and ohms consistently; for example, 20 mA is 0.020 A.

Calculate before connecting

Suppose a 9 V source is connected in series with a 450 Ω resistor and an approximately ohmic load. The ideal current estimate is 9 ÷ 450 = 0.020 A, or 20 mA. This is a calculation, not proof that the load, source, or resistor is rated for that condition. For nonlinear parts such as LEDs, use their datasheet behavior rather than assuming a fixed resistance.

Measure carefully

  1. De-energize the circuit before changing connections.
  2. Use a meter and leads rated for the measurement, and select the correct function and range.
  3. Measure voltage in parallel with the element. Measuring current requires the meter to be placed in series and the correct current input jack; never place a current-mode meter directly across a voltage source.
  4. Compare measured values with the model while considering meter accuracy, supply variation, and component tolerance.
Safety: This example concerns low-voltage educational circuits. It is not guidance for mains or high-energy equipment. Do not probe a circuit unless you understand the hazards and have appropriately rated equipment.

Try the calculator
