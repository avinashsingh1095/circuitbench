Limiting current through an LED

Estimate a series resistor for one LED, then validate the assumptions against the actual parts.

An LED is not a fixed resistor. A series resistor is a simple way to limit current in many low-voltage indicator circuits. The first-order calculation is R = (Vs − Vf) ÷ I, where Vs is supply voltage, Vf is the LED forward voltage at the intended operating current, and I is current in amperes.

Worked estimate

For a 5 V supply, a LED whose datasheet gives 2.0 V at the selected operating point, and a 10 mA target: (5 − 2) ÷ 0.010 = 300 Ω. A 330 Ω value gives an idealized estimate of (5 − 2) ÷ 330 ≈ 9.1 mA. Actual values move with supply tolerance, forward-voltage variation, temperature, and resistor tolerance.

Check resistor dissipation

Estimate resistor power using P = I²R or the voltage across it multiplied by current. Choose a resistor with an appropriate rated power and thermal margin; derating depends on part construction and environment. Do not treat a generic margin as a guarantee of safe operation.

Before powering

  • Check LED polarity and verify forward-voltage/current information from its datasheet.
  • Confirm supply voltage and the maximum current of any pin or driver in the circuit.
  • For multiple LEDs, analyze the series/parallel arrangement and component tolerances; one resistor per branch is often easier to reason about than sharing one across parallel LEDs.
  • Disconnect power before rewiring and inspect for shorts.
