Voltage dividers and loading

Understand what the two-resistor equation predicts—and what it omits.

Connect upper resistor R₁ from the input to the output node, then lower resistor R₂ from that node to ground. With no load attached, the output is Vout = Vin × R₂ ÷ (R₁ + R₂). The series current is Vin ÷ (R₁ + R₂).

Example

For 5 V, R₁ = 1 kΩ, and R₂ = 10 kΩ, the unloaded output is 5 × 10,000 ÷ 11,000 ≈ 4.545 V. The divider current is approximately 0.455 mA. The calculation also lets you estimate each resistor's dissipation using P = I²R.

What a load does

A device connected from the output node to ground is electrically in parallel with R₂. Its input resistance forms a smaller effective lower-leg resistance, reducing the output. If the load is not much higher in resistance than the divider, the unloaded result can be a poor prediction. Input circuits may also draw current in ways that are not represented by a simple fixed resistance.

Use cases and cautions

Dividers can provide a reference or scale a signal when the source impedance and destination input are understood. They are not voltage regulators: output varies with load and input. Check the destination's absolute maximum ratings, normal operating range, protection needs, and resistor tolerances. A divider alone is not always a safe interface.

Explore an unloaded estimate with the voltage divider calculator. It reports no loaded output because no load parameter is requested.

Before use: Confirm the destination circuit requirements in its datasheet and test with a current-limited supply where appropriate.
