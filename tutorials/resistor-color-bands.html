Reading four-band resistors

Read two significant digits, a multiplier, and a tolerance band.

On a common four-band resistor, the first two bands encode significant digits, the third is a multiplier, and the fourth is tolerance. Start from the side where the first band is nearer the end; the tolerance band is often spaced farther away. Do not rely only on spacing if the part is ambiguous.

Example: brown, black, red, gold

Brown is 1 and black is 0, forming 10. Red multiplies by 100, so the nominal value is 10 × 100 = 1,000 Ω (1 kΩ). Gold indicates ±5% in this scheme, giving a nominal range of 950 Ω to 1,050 Ω.

Common digit sequence

Black 0, brown 1, red 2, orange 3, yellow 4, green 5, blue 6, violet 7, gray 8, white 9. Gold and silver commonly appear as multiplier and tolerance bands; their exact permitted positions and meanings depend on the code variant.

Verify the part

Color can be hard to distinguish because of lighting, wear, or vision differences. Use a meter with the circuit de-energized and isolate one lead when parallel paths could affect the reading. Compare the measured value and tolerance with the schematic and part markings. This draft calculator covers four bands, not five- or six-band parts.
