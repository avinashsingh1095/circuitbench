Reading four-band resistors

Read significant digits, the multiplier, and the tolerance band in the correct order.

For a standard four-band resistor, band 1 and band 2 are significant digits, band 3 is the multiplier, and band 4 is tolerance. The first band is usually closer to one end; the tolerance band is commonly spaced farther apart. Confirm the marking scheme from a component document if the bands are unclear—do not guess orientation.

Decode an example

Brown = 1, black = 0, so the significant digits form 10. Red is ×100: 10 × 100 = 1,000 Ω (1 kΩ). Gold tolerance is ±5%. The nominal range is 1,000 × 0.95 to 1,000 × 1.05 = 950 Ω to 1,050 Ω. This is a tolerance interval, not a guarantee of the actual measured value.

Four-band reference

Significant digits: black 0, brown 1, red 2, orange 3, yellow 4, green 5, blue 6, violet 7, gray 8, white 9. Common multipliers include black ×1, brown ×10, red ×100, orange ×1,000, gold ×0.1, and silver ×0.01. Common tolerance bands include brown ±1%, red ±2%, green ±0.5%, blue ±0.25%, violet ±0.1%, gray ±0.05%, gold ±5%, and silver ±10%. This page's calculator supports a four-band set, not every special code or five-/six-band format.

Verify a physical part

  1. Turn the circuit off and safely discharge stored energy according to the equipment instructions.
  2. Read the bands in good light; color perception, fading, and coating can make colors ambiguous.
  3. Measure resistance only when unpowered. Parallel circuit paths affect in-circuit readings; isolate one resistor lead if the schematic and safe procedure permit.
  4. Compare the measured value and expected tolerance with the schematic or manufacturer marking.
