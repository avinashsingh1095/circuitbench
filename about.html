About

CircuitBench for practical electronics learning

CircuitBench brings together quick calculations and beginner-friendly learning for the kinds of circuit questions that come up in student projects, maker work, and day-to-day electronics troubleshooting.

What the site offers

At a practical level, CircuitBench helps visitors estimate values for common tasks such as choosing a resistor for an LED, checking current through a loop, sizing a power resistor, decoding a resistor value from its bands, and understanding how a simple voltage divider behaves in an unloaded circuit.

The site also includes tutorial-style articles on Ohm's law, voltage dividers, resistor power ratings, Arduino GPIO and analog input basics, ESP32 input limits, PWM behaviour, and simple troubleshooting methods using a multimeter.

Who it is for

CircuitBench is intended for students, hobbyists, and self-directed learners who want a quick, understandable way to check their assumptions before wiring or measuring a circuit. The content is designed to be clear and useful without requiring a deep electronics background, while still encouraging careful verification against component datasheets and the actual hardware in use.

How the site works

The calculators run entirely in the visitor's browser using JavaScript. They are a convenient starting point for calculations, but they do not replace the component ratings, board documentation, datasheets, or practical testing that a real project requires.

All pages use a lightweight static structure with plain HTML, CSS, and JavaScript. The site does not depend on a server-side database, user accounts, or a backend application. A hosting provider may still record basic technical request information, and that is described in the privacy policy.
